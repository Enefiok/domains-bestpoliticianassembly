WHY DO WE INVITES STATE ACTORS DURING OUR SUMMITS

During the Best Politicians Summit, State actors’ matter because they’re the only ones who can turn a decision into something that’s legally binding, funded, and enforced across a whole territory. Everyone else can influence, but state actors execute.

Here’s why they’re central:

Execution Power

Legally Binding Authority

01

They have the monopoly on legitimate force and law

Only state actors can:

  • • Pass laws that apply to everyone in that jurisdiction
  • • Use police, courts, and military to enforce decisions
  • • Levy taxes, seize assets, detain people
02

They control resources and budgets

State actors decide:

  • • Where public money goes - roads, schools, health, security
  • • Who gets contracts, licenses, appointments
  • • Which sectors get subsidies, tax breaks, or regulation
03

Market & Legal Alignment

Businesses don’t just react to demand. They react to what INEC, NERC, CAC, state assemblies, and governors decide is legal. If you’re wrong about what the state will do, your strategy fails regardless of market conditions.

04

They manage external relations and risk

Only state actors can sign treaties, negotiate with other states, declare emergencies, or request military intervention.

That means they control exposure to foreign policy shifts, sanctions, security deployments, and international funding. For investors and citizens, that’s the biggest source of political risk.

05

They coordinate fragmented interests

Society has conflicting demands: workers vs employers, farmers vs herders, urban vs rural.

State actors are supposed to aggregate those demands, make trade-offs, and produce a single policy direction. When they fail at this, you get policy paralysis or parallel power structures.

06

They create policy continuity and legitimacy

Decisions by state actors carry legal continuity. A new governor can change priorities, but they inherit the state’s debt, contracts, and institutions.

That continuity matters for long-term projects like infrastructure, education, and security. No private actor can provide it at scale.

WHY POLITICAL CAREER

The choice to train and pursue a career in politics is driven by several key motivations, including:

Translating passion into structured legislative action.

Many are motivated by a desire to improve their communities. Training in fields like public policy or political science helps translate this passion into tangible, well-structured legislative action.

Political training helps advocates learn how to navigate government structures so they can effectively voice the concerns of marginalized groups.
